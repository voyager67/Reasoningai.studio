# Research Grant Writing

## Purpose

Translate a research plan into a funder-aligned proposal without overstating readiness, impact, or preliminary evidence.

## Input

Bring any material already available: funding call, eligibility rules, proposal template, research concept, preliminary data, team capabilities, budget, and duration. If an essential item is absent, state the gap and proceed with a clearly labelled provisional plan rather than repeatedly asking for information that is already present.

## Workflow

1. Read the current call and extract eligibility, evaluation criteria, page limits, permitted costs, milestones, and required attachments.
2. Map the research problem to the funder’s stated priorities without changing the underlying evidence or inventing alignment.
3. Write a concise central hypothesis or objective and a limited set of aims with measurable success criteria.
4. Connect methods, data, infrastructure, team expertise, and deliverables to each aim.
5. Separate preliminary evidence, published evidence, assumptions, and expected outcomes.
6. Build a milestone plan with dependencies, risks, alternative approaches, and realistic resource estimates.
7. Explain broader impact through a plausible pathway and measurable beneficiaries; avoid guaranteed societal claims.
8. Audit budget justification, data management, ethics, dissemination, and evaluation plans against the call.

## Required output

Funder-aligned proposal draft; aims page; work plan; milestone/risk table; budget narrative; data-management summary; and a compliance matrix against the call.

## Integrity and safety gates

- Never invent preliminary results, collaborators, facilities, letters of support, or institutional approvals.
- Do not promise outcomes outside the team’s control.
- Clearly distinguish preliminary evidence from future work.
- Use the current official call as the authority for all submission requirements.
