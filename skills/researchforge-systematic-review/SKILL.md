# Systematic Review

## Purpose

Keep review decisions traceable from the question through the final synthesis. Use a systematic-review label only when the work meets the corresponding methodological standard.

## Input

Bring any material already available: research question, protocol or draft, databases, eligibility criteria, screening team, access constraints, and review type. If an essential item is absent, state the gap and proceed with a clearly labelled provisional plan rather than repeatedly asking for information that is already present.

## Workflow

1. Choose and justify the review type: systematic, scoping, rapid, evidence map, or narrative. Match the method to the question.
2. Specify eligibility criteria, information sources, search strategy, selection process, data items, risk-of-bias approach, and synthesis plan before screening.
3. Pilot the search and screening process; amend the protocol with dated reasons instead of silently changing criteria.
4. Maintain deduplication and screening logs, including reasons for full-text exclusions and records that could not be retrieved.
5. Extract study characteristics and outcomes using a piloted form. Double-check high-impact fields.
6. Assess risk of bias using a tool appropriate to the study design; do not replace it with an informal quality score.
7. Synthesize narratively when measures or populations are not meaningfully comparable. Pool quantitatively only when assumptions and heterogeneity permit.
8. Report flow counts, deviations, funding/conflicts, limitations, and search coverage according to the current reporting guidance.

## Required output

Protocol; full search strings and dates; selection and exclusion log; extraction schema; risk-of-bias rationale; synthesis; deviations; and a reporting checklist. Mark each PRISMA item as reported, not applicable, or missing.

## Integrity and safety gates

- Never fabricate screening counts, protocol registration, or a PRISMA flow.
- Do not pool incompatible outcomes solely to produce a meta-analysis.
- Do not treat absence of evidence as evidence of no effect.
- Check the current official reporting guideline rather than relying on memory.
