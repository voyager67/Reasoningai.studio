# Evidence Matrix Synthesis

## Purpose

Make cross-paper patterns visible while retaining study-level differences and the context behind each result.

## Input

Bring any material already available: paper cards, full texts or extracts, review question, inclusion rules, and target synthesis format. If an essential item is absent, state the gap and proceed with a clearly labelled provisional plan rather than repeatedly asking for information that is already present.

## Workflow

1. Define the synthesis question and units of comparison. Avoid combining studies that address different estimands.
2. Build a matrix with study identity, context, method, dataset/sample, comparator, outcome, direction, uncertainty, limitations, and source anchors.
3. Group evidence by mechanism, population, method, outcome, and context; do not group only by whether a result is positive or negative.
4. Identify consistent patterns, contradictions, null findings, boundary conditions, and missing evidence.
5. Investigate apparent contradictions using design differences, measurement, data quality, sample size, and publication/version differences.
6. Rank conclusions by directness, consistency, risk of bias, precision, and applicability; avoid a single opaque quality score.
7. Draft a narrative that makes each cross-study claim traceable to individual studies and exceptions.

## Required output

Evidence matrix (CSV/Markdown); thematic map; agreement/contradiction table; evidence-strength narrative; unresolved questions; and claim-to-study links.

## Integrity and safety gates

- Do not treat paper count as evidence strength.
- Do not count multiple analyses of one dataset as independent replications.
- Keep null and negative findings in the synthesis.
- State when the corpus is a convenience sample or incomplete.

## Related skills

## Example request

> Synthesize 20 included studies and show where results agree, conflict, and depend on dataset or metric choices.
