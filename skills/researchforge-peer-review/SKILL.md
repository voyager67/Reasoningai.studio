# Peer Review

## Purpose

Assess the manuscript as submitted, not as an imagined stronger paper. Distinguish fatal validity issues from fixable presentation problems.

## Input

Bring any material already available: manuscript, supplementary material, reviewer criteria, venue scope, and conflict-of-interest or confidentiality constraints. If an essential item is absent, state the gap and proceed with a clearly labelled provisional plan rather than repeatedly asking for information that is already present.

## Workflow

1. Summarize the paper’s question, approach, and claimed contribution neutrally before evaluating it.
2. Assess significance and fit against the venue scope and literature, noting uncertainty about novelty where search coverage is limited.
3. Audit method validity, data suitability, baseline fairness, statistical reasoning, result-to-claim alignment, and reproducibility.
4. Check ethical approvals, data rights, participant privacy, dual-use risks, and disclosure requirements where applicable.
5. Classify concerns as major, moderate, or minor and explain the consequence for the paper’s conclusions.
6. For each major concern, propose a feasible path to resolution or explain why it cannot be repaired within the current study.
7. Identify strengths and any evidence that supports the contribution; do not let one weakness erase unrelated valid contributions.
8. Provide an overall assessment with confidence and scope limitations.

## Required output

Structured review: summary; strengths; major concerns; minor concerns; ethics/reproducibility; questions to authors; overall recommendation rationale; and reviewer confidence.

## Integrity and safety gates

- Do not disclose confidential manuscript contents outside the review task.
- Do not fabricate references or demand irrelevant citations to the reviewer’s own work.
- Do not infer misconduct from an unexplained result alone.
- Separate verified defects from suspicions and stylistic preferences.
