# Anti-Defensive Writing

## Purpose

Make the argument direct and confident because the evidence is clear—not because weaknesses have been hidden. A research paper is an evidence-led argument, not a project diary or a sales pitch.

## Input

Bring any material already available: draft passage or manuscript, tables/figures, claim-evidence ledger if available, target venue, and author intent. If an essential item is absent, state the gap and proceed with a clearly labelled provisional plan rather than repeatedly asking for information that is already present.

## Workflow

1. Identify the paragraph’s job: establish a problem, explain a gap, state a contribution, report an observation, interpret a result, or disclose a limitation.
2. Mark defensive patterns: unnecessary apologies, repeated hedges, chronology of abandoned attempts, generic “it is important to note” phrases, and self-criticism broader than the data.
3. Separate unnecessary rhetoric from material facts. Preserve negative, null, mixed, and failed results whenever they are relevant to the research question or interpretation.
4. Rewrite around a precise claim and the evidence that supports it. State conditions, dataset, comparator, metric, and uncertainty where needed.
5. Replace vague self-judgment with specific limitation language: what was not tested, why it matters, and what conclusion remains supported.
6. Do not create a more favorable comparison frame by changing metrics, omitting relevant baselines, or redefining the task after seeing results.
7. Check whether the revision alters scope, strength, causal language, or certainty. Flag substantive changes for author approval.
8. Return a concise explanation of edits and any unresolved evidence issue.

## Required output

Revised text; a compact before/after rationale; a list of retained limitations/negative results; and any claims that still need evidence. When asked, provide tracked-change style edits.

## Integrity and safety gates

- Never hide, delete, or downplay a material negative result to make the paper look stronger.
- Never advise selective reporting, metric shopping, misleading baselines, or omission of relevant comparisons.
- Never turn “not evaluated” into “not a limitation” or imply generalization beyond the tested scope.
- Do not inflate confidence or replace one broad hedge with an unsupported absolute statement.
- If the evidence is weak, say so plainly and help narrow the claim.
